# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a structured, iterative project management approach designed to turn customer and business needs into measurable value through clear ownership, disciplined execution, and continuous learning. The methodology emphasizes customer-first thinking, small incremental delivery, and data-informed decisions so teams can adapt as requirements, dependencies, and risk evolve. Across the full lifecycle of a project, OctoAcme keeps communication transparent, responsibilities explicit, and quality standards visible to both delivery teams and stakeholders.

## Core Principles

- Customer-first: prioritize customer value, usability, and business impact
- Iterative delivery: break work into small, testable increments that can be reviewed and adjusted
- Clear ownership: assign accountable roles for delivery, prioritization, and approvals
- Data-informed decisions: measure outcomes, track metrics, and iterate based on evidence
- Psychological safety: encourage feedback, learning, and candid problem solving

## Project Lifecycle

1. Initiation — validate the problem, align stakeholders, define success criteria, and decide whether to proceed
2. Planning — create a prioritized backlog, estimate work, define milestones, and identify dependencies
3. Execution & Tracking — build the work, monitor progress, manage blockers, and maintain quality
4. Release & Deployment — verify readiness, ship the change, and confirm production impact
5. Close & Retrospective — capture lessons learned, track action items, and improve future delivery

## Summary of OctoAcme Processes

OctoAcme organizes work around a disciplined yet adaptable project lifecycle. Initiation focuses on validating the need, identifying stakeholders, documenting the problem and success metrics, and making the decision to proceed. Planning then turns that validated idea into a backlog with clear owners, estimates, dependencies, milestones, and acceptance criteria. During execution, the team maintains a regular cadence of standups, progress review, QA, and risk management while escalating blockers appropriately. Release work standardizes deployment readiness, smoke testing, verification, and communication so that changes can be shipped with less uncertainty.

The operating model relies on defined roles and responsibilities rather than fragmented ownership. Developers implement and validate work, product leaders define outcomes and trade-offs, and project managers coordinate timelines, dependencies, stakeholder updates, and risk mitigation. Communication is intentionally regular and structured—using daily standups, weekly syncs, stakeholder updates, milestone reviews, and escalation paths—to keep everyone aligned. Quality is embedded throughout the process through code and integration testing, security scanning, acceptance criteria, and release checklists. Finally, retrospectives and continuous improvement practices ensure that the team learns from each sprint or release and converts improvement opportunities into concrete action items.
